WHAT COUNTS AS A SKILL
A skill is a specific named thing a candidate must know or use: a language, framework, library, tool, platform, protocol, certification, methodology, technical domain or concept, or named technique or deliverable (for example "python", "kubernetes", "grpc", "agile", "distributed systems", "deep learning", "manual testing", "wireframes", "portfolio").
Do NOT extract degrees, years of experience, soft skills (communication, teamwork, problem-solving), or general duties.

RULES
1. Use only what is in the text; never invent or infer a skill, and never expand an abbreviation (keep "sast" as "sast"). Keep the original wording, dropping only filler such as "experience with" or "knowledge of". Write "restful apis" / "rest apis" as "rest" and "graphql apis" as "graphql"; keep other phrases such as "third-party apis" as written.
2. Skip category labels ("languages", "cloud & devops"). Keep only the skills listed under them.
3. If a generic category is followed by examples ("such as", "like", or in brackets), extract the examples ("version control systems (git, github)" -> "git", "github"). If the term before the brackets is itself a specific skill, keep it too ("agile methodologies (scrum, kanban)" -> "agile", "scrum", "kanban"; "cross-platform testing (android, ios)" -> "cross-platform testing", "android", "ios").
4. Split lists separated by commas, pipes, line breaks, "and" or "&". Split a slash only when both sides are named technologies ("kotlin/java", "agile/scrum"); keep other slash terms whole ("ci/cd", "tcp/ip", "ui/ux", "html/css", "c/c++", "hardware/software"). Keep well-known multi-word names whole ("apache flink", "spring boot").
5. Join a line with the next one only when the first clearly stops mid-phrase ("message" then "queues" -> "message queues"). Never merge separate list items.
6. Keep related but distinct skills ("oci", "oci vault"). No exact duplicates; keep first-appearance order.
7. From a sentence, extract the named tools plus the specific practice, domain or artifact it requires, as a short noun phrase ("experience designing software architecture" -> "software architecture"; "writing test cases" -> "test cases"; "user-centered design principles" -> "user-centered design"). Skip a sentence only if it names nothing a candidate could be searched on ("attention to detail", "meeting deadlines").
8. Keep a qualifier that makes a term specific ("web ui/ux", "mobile architecture", "cross-platform testing"), and keep named work artifacts or credentials a candidate must bring ("portfolio", "wireframes", "certification").
9. Copy spelling exactly as it appears in the text, even if it looks like a typo.
